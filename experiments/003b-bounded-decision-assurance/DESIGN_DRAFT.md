# Experiment 003B — Bounded Decision Assurance Challenge

**Status:** design draft; not preregistered; not frozen; no results exist  
**Purpose:** test Action Protocol 0.2 against strong transparent baselines under conditions that can falsify the reform

## Primary question

Does typed uncertainty plus a bounded assurance-and-learning case reduce harmful false assurance under signed consequence uncertainty, shared structural omission, source dependence, stakeholder conflict, and distribution shift without excessive deferral or oracle information?

## Contributions this experiment must distinguish

1. correct treatment of signed consequence uncertainty;
2. better exploration of uncertain values and rankings;
3. detection of structural omissions;
4. explicit protection of affected groups and non-compensable constraints;
5. useful choice between action, pilot, learning, and deferral;
6. honest bounded-status assignment; and
7. benefit of the integrated method beyond its strongest component.

## Candidate arms

| Arm | Description |
|---|---|
| B0 | Named default/baseline action |
| B1 | Raw point-estimate expected utility |
| B2 | Action 0.1 signed confidence shrinkage |
| B3 | Minimax regret over declared models |
| B4 | SMAA-like stochastic consequence and weight exploration |
| B5 | RDM-like vulnerability testing across declared scenarios |
| B6 | Evidence-failure trajectory audit without broader assurance layers |
| T1 | Bounded Decision Assurance 0.2 |
| A1–A6 | T1 ablations removing provenance, structural challenge, stakeholder constraints, tail metric, adaptive learning, or assurance-deficit gate one at a time |

Every algorithm receives the same non-oracle input packet. Method-specific information must be either computable from that packet or charged as an elicitation cost.

## Generator structure

Each case contains:

- 4–8 action paths, including at least one baseline and sometimes a learn-first or pilot option;
- 3–6 stakeholders with unequal exposure;
- 3–7 criteria with controlled overlap and interactions;
- 3–8 visible scenarios;
- 0–3 hidden structural scenarios generated by a different topology, mechanism, or source family;
- correlated evidence sources with explicit ancestry;
- consequence distributions containing positive, negative, mixed-sign, skewed, and rare-tail outcomes;
- value models ranging from nearly shared to sharply conflicting;
- reversibility, delay cost, lock-in, monitoring noise, and trigger latency;
- measurement gaming and implementation-failure conditions; and
- a complete oracle used only for scoring.

## Experimental factors

1. **Confidence calibration:** calibrated, uninformative, sign-correlated, prestige-correlated, adversarial.
2. **Omission type:** none, missing tail state, missing causal interaction, missing affected group, missing temporal threshold.
3. **Source dependence:** independent, clustered, monocultural, incorrectly labeled.
4. **Value conflict:** low, compensable, non-compensable, preference interaction violating additive independence.
5. **Tail regime:** absent, thin, rare/severe, ambiguous probability.
6. **Learning value:** none, low, high, misleading proxy.
7. **Reversibility:** genuine, costly, delayed, illusory.
8. **Shift:** none, covariate, mechanism, stakeholder composition, strategic response.

## Outcomes

### Primary

- harmful false-assurance rate;
- true expected welfare at matched deferral/intervention cost;
- lower-tail loss or CVaR;
- worst-group shortfall;
- total regret, including delay and information costs.

### Secondary

- scenario/hazard discovery recall and false-positive rate for seeded omissions;
- rank acceptability and interval calibration;
- non-compensable constraint violations;
- appropriate deferral and inappropriate deferral;
- net value of information and trigger timeliness;
- sensitivity to scenario-set generator;
- reproducibility of case status;
- compute, analyst-input, and review burden.

### Diagnostic

- evidence-family failure trajectory;
- criterion double-counting sensitivity;
- status changes by ablation;
- performance broken down by omission, calibration, shift, and value-conflict regime.

## Candidate preregistered hypotheses

- **H1 Sign coherence:** Lower evidence quality alone never makes a fixed negative consequence less severe in T1's central representation.
- **H2 False assurance:** T1 reduces harmful false-assurance rate relative to the strongest baseline at matched deferral cost.
- **H3 Tail protection:** T1 improves CVaR without exceeding a preregistered mean-regret margin.
- **H4 Distributional protection:** T1 reduces worst-group shortfall and constraint violations in active-conflict cells.
- **H5 Structural challenge:** T1 improves seeded-omission recall over generic scenario enumeration without an unacceptable false-positive burden.
- **H6 Learning:** T1 chooses information-gathering or adaptive paths with higher oracle net benefit than fixed-action baselines.
- **H7 Integration:** T1 exceeds every ablation on a preregistered multi-outcome dominance rule; otherwise the full architecture is not justified.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

1. Beating B2 alone is not success because B2 has a known signed-confidence defect.
2. T1 fails the central claim if it does not beat the strongest simple baseline at matched deferral or total process cost.
3. A reduction in tail harm is not success if it is bought through excessive deferral, baseline selection, or worst-group harm.
4. An omitted-state method is not credited for information encoded in a privileged generator label or oracle distribution.
5. If the challenge layer floods the process with false hazards, its review cost counts against it.
6. If the integrated method does not beat a component or ablation, prefer the simpler method.
7. No synthetic result establishes real-world efficacy or legitimacy.

## Anti-overfitting design

The public development generator and seeds are used for debugging only. Confirmatory evaluation must use a sealed generator variant, sealed seeds, or an independently specified case family. The candidate implementation, configuration, analysis code, and hypotheses are frozen before protected execution. Detailed protected failures are released once and then become development data.

## Analysis outline

- paired cases and common random numbers across arms;
- effect sizes with bootstrap or randomization intervals specified before execution;
- multiplicity control or a hierarchical decision rule for the primary family;
- equivalence/non-inferiority margins for mean welfare and cost;
- all factors and interactions reported, including failures;
- no post-hoc composite score promoted to primary status;
- deterministic replication and artifact hash manifest.

## Human-factors companion study

A later 003C or 005 pilot should compare interfaces that show:

1. recommendation first;
2. evidence and deficits before recommendation;
3. performance vector without a recommendation; and
4. existing unaided practice.

Capture the participant's pre-advice judgment, post-advice judgment, verification behavior, appropriate acceptance/rejection, understanding of scope, and time cost. Do not infer human benefit from algorithmic simulation.
