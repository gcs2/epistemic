# SES Experimental Program Roadmap

## Purpose

Experiment 001 tests a single mathematical mechanism under known truth. The broader claim—that structured, plural, adversarial synthesis improves real truth-finding—requires successively less artificial tests. This roadmap defines that escalation without treating one synthetic victory as validation of the whole system.

## Evidence ladder

| Stage | Study | Truth access | Primary threat addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Synthetic dependence benchmark | Exact generating truth | Mathematical and implementation failure |
| 2 | Provenance recovery benchmark | Curated source ancestry | Inability to identify real dependence |
| 3 | Blinded retrospective wind tunnel | Known outcome hidden behind cutoff | Hindsight, hypothesis omission, narrative fitting |
| 4 | Prospective forecasting tournament | Future resolution | Calibration under genuine uncertainty |
| 5 | Decision-linked field studies | Consequences of live decisions | External validity and institutional behavior |

No stage inherits validity automatically from the prior stage.

### Current position — 2026-09-04

- Stage 1 / Experiment 001: complete and deterministically reproduced.
- Stage 2A / generated provenance recovery: complete and deterministically reproduced. Six of eight preregistered hypotheses were supported; the two failed hypotheses exposed false-merger risk in unconditional hybrid clustering.
- Stage 2B / adjudicated real corpus: protocol drafted; corpus acquisition and independent adjudicators remain outstanding.
- Stage 3A / generated decision stress test: complete and awaiting final deterministic artifact verification. Four of seven hypotheses were supported; the action-0.1 confidence rule, registered gate criterion, minimax rule, and distributional test all exposed limitations.
- Stage 3B: a comparative Bounded Decision Assurance challenge is drafted but not preregistered or executed. The Action 0.2 reference engine now provides an implementation target; its existence is not evidence that the method works.
- Stages 3–5 beyond that draft: designed but not executed.

### Experiment 003A finding boundary

The generated decision benchmark showed that declared agreement can share an omitted tail; multiplying low-confidence negative impacts by confidence can increase severe-downside exposure; and a gate can lower tail exposure while imposing excessive regret through baseline deferral. The registered minimax view set did not improve P90 regret. The affected-party threshold never activated, so the experiment cannot support a distributional-safety conclusion.

Action protocol 0.2 must remove signed confidence multiplication, represent impact uncertainty explicitly, label all robustness as conditional on the scenario set, and require an open-world tail challenge. Experiment 003B must generate real stakeholder tradeoffs and use continuous distributional loss before retrospective or field testing.

Deep research now adds three requirements: keep consequence, value, model, evidence, and process uncertainty typed; separate the decision-performance case from its epistemic-assurance case; and protect confirmatory evaluation from recursive benchmark overfitting. See [`../research/deep/001-bounded-robustness/report-source.md`](../research/deep/001-bounded-robustness/report-source.md) and the [`003B design draft`](003b-bounded-decision-assurance/DESIGN_DRAFT.md).

## Experiment 002 — Provenance and dependence recovery

Experiment 002 is divided into 002A, the completed generated-document pilot, and 002B, the pending adjudicated real-corpus study. See `002-provenance/REAL_CORPUS_PROTOCOL.md`.

### Question

Can human, algorithmic, and hybrid teams reconstruct the effective independence structure of evidence accurately enough to improve synthesis?

### Materials

Construct a benchmark of source families from domains with traceable ancestry:

- systematic reviews containing overlapping studies;
- news stories derived from common wire reports;
- reports reusing the same administrative dataset;
- model outputs conditioned on the same upstream summary;
- historical works sharing one archival document; and
- deliberately generated citation-laundering trees.

Each benchmark item contains claims and public source metadata. A sealed gold graph is adjudicated by at least two domain researchers plus a provenance methodologist. Disagreements remain labeled rather than forced.

### Arms

1. Raw citation-count baseline.
2. Text-similarity clustering.
3. Citation-graph reconstruction.
4. Single research agent.
5. Multiple same-family agents.
6. Heterogeneous agents with isolated scouting.
7. Human provenance analyst.
8. Human-agent hybrid with SES artifact schema.

### Primary measures

- pairwise dependency classification precision, recall, and F1;
- graph edge calibration when probabilities are allowed;
- effective-source-count error;
- downstream Brier change when recovered groups are used;
- analyst time and compute cost; and
- false independence rate, treated as more dangerous than false grouping in high-stakes settings.

### Key falsifier

If recoverable dependence remains too inaccurate to improve downstream forecasts, SES must use interval or worst-case dependence rather than point grouping.

## Experiment 003 — Blinded retrospective epistemic wind tunnel

### Question

Does the full SES workflow improve probabilistic accuracy, hypothesis recall, causal adequacy, and correction compared with simpler human and agent workflows when all post-cutoff information is hidden?

### Case portfolio

Target 60–120 cases, balanced across:

- geopolitics and conflict;
- public health;
- technology adoption and safety incidents;
- economics and policy;
- environmental systems;
- historical causal controversies with adjudicable intermediate outcomes; and
- deliberately synthetic open-world cases with an omitted-hypothesis trap.

Every case receives:

- a question and resolution criterion written without outcome language;
- a fixed evidentiary cutoff;
- an archived pre-cutoff source corpus;
- a sealed outcome and adjudication record;
- a source-provenance graph or uncertainty set; and
- domain-expert review for anachronisms.

Cases must be selected by a team isolated from the teams running study arms. Case inclusion criteria are frozen before forecasts are generated.

### Experimental arms

| Arm | Workflow |
|---|---|
| A | Unstructured single-agent research and answer |
| B | Structured single-agent inquiry contract and evidence ledger |
| C | Multi-agent debate without source isolation or dependence mapping |
| D | SES roles and adversarial review without dependence adjustment |
| E | SES roles plus dependence-aware deterministic synthesis |
| F | Human expert panel using its normal professional process |
| G | Human-agent SES hybrid with explicit acceptance checkpoints |

Where feasible, use a factorial subdesign to isolate independent scouting, model advocacy, red-teaming, and dependence mapping instead of comparing only bundled workflows.

### Blinding and contamination controls

- Remove post-cutoff references and metadata.
- Block network access or serve a controlled archive.
- Screen model pretraining contamination with impossible-date controls and counterfactual identifiers.
- Require verbatim evidence locators for every outcome-relevant claim.
- Record model provider, version, system instructions, sampling parameters, tools, and context construction.
- Prevent advocates from observing one another before independent first-round submissions.
- Keep outcome adjudicators blind to study arm.

### Outcomes

**Primary:** Brier loss on preregistered probabilistic resolutions.

**Secondary:**

- log loss and calibration;
- hypothesis recall, including whether the true/best-supported hypothesis entered the live set;
- source authenticity and provenance accuracy;
- causal-model scoring by blinded domain reviewers;
- correction between initial and final forecasts;
- confidently wrong rate;
- time, financial cost, tokens, and energy proxy;
- report legibility and user comprehension; and
- minority-report preservation.

### Analysis

Use hierarchical models with case and domain random effects and prespecified arm contrasts. Compare cost-effectiveness frontiers, not only raw accuracy. Estimate the contribution of case difficulty, source dependence, model family, and outcome base rate. Publish every registered case and arm unless excluded by a frozen rule.

### Sample-size rule

Run a labeled pilot used only to estimate case and team variance. Freeze the confirmatory sample size through simulation-based power analysis before revealing confirmatory outcomes. Do not reuse pilot cases in the confirmatory set.

## Experiment 004 — Prospective forecasting tournament

### Question

Does SES remain calibrated when neither researchers nor models can access the outcome, and does it update more effectively than competing workflows as evidence arrives?

### Design

- 200 or more objectively resolvable questions across 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month horizons.
- Questions written and adjudicated by an independent resolution committee.
- Forecasts timestamped and versioned.
- Equal information windows or recorded source-access differences.
- Scoring by time-averaged Brier, terminal Brier, log score, and calibration-sharpness decomposition.
- Frozen policy for question cancellation, ambiguity, and exogenous resolution changes.

The tournament should compare initial forecasts, update frequency, update magnitude, and whether updates track genuinely diagnostic evidence rather than attention volume.

### Critical analysis

Forecasting performance is domain- and horizon-specific. Contributor or agent weights may be learned only within sufficiently supported strata. No universal “truth score” or permanent expert caste should be produced.

## Experiment 005 — Decision-linked field studies

### Question

Does SES improve decisions, not merely predictions, when values, organizational incentives, and implementation constraints matter?

### Candidate settings

- research-priority selection;
- institutional risk registers;
- public-health resource allocation;
- journal or funder evidence reviews;
- technology deployment reviews; and
- citizen-assembly briefing processes.

### Design principles

- Separate empirical posterior, value judgments, and decision rule.
- Register the counterfactual decision process: what would have occurred without SES?
- Measure delay, implementation fidelity, reversibility, distributional effects, and participant trust.
- Use stepped-wedge or matched rollout designs where randomization is legitimate.
- Maintain appeal and minority-report mechanisms.

Field deployment requires governance and domain-specific ethics review. Prediction gains do not authorize political decisions.

## Cross-study ablation matrix

The program should eventually estimate the marginal contribution of:

1. inquiry-contract precision;
2. independent evidence scouting;
3. provenance mapping;
4. explicit alternative hypotheses;
5. disciplinary translation;
6. causal modeling;
7. adversarial review;
8. deterministic aggregation;
9. human acceptance checkpoints;
10. external audit; and
11. prospective calibration weights.

Interactions matter. Red-teaming may help only when hypotheses are independently generated; model pluralism may hurt when every model derives from the same sources.

## Governance before human or live deployment

Before Experiments 003–005:

- determine whether institutional ethics review is required;
- create participant consent and compensation policies;
- establish private-source and personal-data handling;
- publish conflicts of interest and funding;
- appoint an independent outcome-resolution and appeals committee;
- define handling for dangerous or defamatory conclusions;
- preserve audit logs without exposing protected sources; and
- commit to publishing negative and null results.

## Advancement criteria

SES advances to higher-stakes deployment only if it demonstrates:

- calibration improvement or equivalent accuracy at meaningfully lower cost;
- explicit detection of conditions where its own method hurts performance;
- reproducibility by an independent implementation;
- preservation of dissent and traceable evidence;
- comprehensibility to affected non-specialists; and
- governance proportional to consequence.

Failure at a stage should narrow or revise the claimed domain, not trigger a search for more favorable metrics.
