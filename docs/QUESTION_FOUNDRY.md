# The Question Foundry

## Selecting consequential inquiries and extracting truth gems

SES should not wait passively for questions. Question selection is itself an epistemic and moral act: it allocates attention, determines which uncertainties become legible, and can make some futures easier to imagine than others.

The Question Foundry is the layer that discovers, reformulates, scores, and portfolios inquiries before the synthesis cycle begins.

## 1. What makes a question valuable?

The most important question is not necessarily the best immediate research question. “How should humanity live?” has immense significance but poor resolution criteria. A narrow question can be answered cleanly yet change nothing. The best SES questions lie near a frontier where importance, uncertainty, tractability, and action meet.

A useful expected-value formulation is:

\[
V(Q)=P(\text{material learning}\mid Q)
\times \text{stakes}
\times P(\text{learning changes action})
\times \text{durability}
\times \text{spillover}
- \text{costs and harms}.
\]

This should guide judgment rather than create a false universal ranking.

## 2. Eligibility gates

Before scoring, a candidate question must pass six gates.

1. **Truth-object clarity:** Is it empirical, causal, predictive, interpretive, normative, or strategic?
2. **Resolution:** Can we describe what evidence would answer or materially narrow it?
3. **Hypothesis discrimination:** Can plausible observations favor one live model over another?
4. **Scope and horizon:** Are population, cutoff, boundary conditions, and time horizon explicit?
5. **Ethical researchability:** Can it be investigated without disproportionate harm, rights violations, or illegitimate authority?
6. **Non-triviality:** Would the result teach more than a restatement of definitions or current consensus?

A failed gate does not mean the topic is unimportant. It means the question must be decomposed or reframed.

## 3. The Question Value Card

Score each dimension from 0 to 5 and preserve the rationale, not merely the total.

| Dimension | Central test |
|---|---|
| Significance | How large are the human, ecological, intellectual, or cultural stakes? |
| Decision leverage | Is a real choice likely to change if the answer changes? |
| Live uncertainty | Do credible models make meaningfully different predictions? |
| Tractability | Can accessible evidence or prospective observation narrow the issue? |
| Discriminability | Are there tests that separate the leading models? |
| Consilience potential | Can independent disciplines or methods converge on it? |
| Learning spillover | Would the result transfer to other important questions? |
| Neglectedness | Is valuable evidence or synthesis missing despite the stakes? |
| Timeliness | Is there a closing decision window or rapidly changing condition? |
| Calibration value | Can part of the question resolve and improve future weighting? |
| Inclusion quality | Can affected and situated knowledge be represented legitimately? |
| Adversarial resilience | Can the inquiry withstand strategic manipulation and politicization? |

Red flags are recorded separately: privacy risk, dual-use risk, defamatory potential, value laundering, irreducible ambiguity, and likely institutional capture.

## 4. Canonical question grammar

A strong SES inquiry can usually be rewritten as:

> Given evidence available by **cutoff C**, for **population/system P** over **horizon T**, which among **hypotheses H** best explains or predicts **outcome Y**, through which mechanisms and boundary conditions, and what feasible observation would most change the ranking?

For strategic questions, add:

> Under explicitly stated values and constraints, which intervention is robust across the plausible models, and where would it cause irreversible downside?

For counterfactuals, add:

> What is the smallest historically and psychologically coherent intervention, how do other actors respond endogenously, and which consequences remain stable across plausible worlds?

## 5. Question decomposition

Civilizational questions should be represented as trees rather than forced into one synthesis packet.

```text
Umbrella question
  -> descriptive baseline
  -> causal mechanisms
  -> forecastable indicators
  -> intervention comparison
  -> distributional effects
  -> normative tradeoffs
  -> monitoring and update plan
```

Empirical and normative branches must meet again before a decision, but they should not be numerically blended as though a value were a measurement.

## 6. Ten high-significance topic programs

These are deliberately cross-disciplinary. Each is an umbrella program whose first task is to create smaller, resolvable inquiry packets.

### 1. Collective intelligence and epistemic institutions

**Question:** Which institutional designs most reliably transform diverse, partially dependent, and adversarial information into calibrated collective beliefs and corrigible decisions?

**Disciplines:** epistemology, forecasting, organizational science, political science, statistics, sociology, computer science, journalism, and Indigenous/community knowledge studies.

**Why it matters:** This is a force multiplier for every other topic. A society that improves its error-correction capacity improves its odds across many futures.

**Possible truth gems:** conditions under which diversity becomes error correction; when deliberation outperforms aggregation; how independence is lost; which forms of dissent predict later correction.

**Discriminating evidence:** blinded comparisons among expert panels, prediction markets, deliberative groups, structured analytic techniques, and SES-like teams on questions with later resolution.

### 2. War, security dilemmas, and durable de-escalation

**Question:** Which observable configurations cause rival states or groups to cross from manageable competition into self-reinforcing escalation, and which interventions reliably reduce danger without creating incentives for aggression?

**Disciplines:** history, international relations, psychology, game theory, economics, network science, anthropology, and military studies.

**Why it matters:** Conflict destroys lives directly and disables cooperation on every global problem.

**Possible truth gems:** coalition-trigger thresholds; credible reassurance conditions; early-warning combinations; circumstances in which deterrence stabilizes or accelerates escalation.

**Discriminating evidence:** preregistered historical case comparisons, diplomatic archives, crisis simulations, and prospective forecasting of defined escalation indicators.

### 3. Artificial intelligence, human control, and distributed benefit

**Question:** Which technical, organizational, economic, and governance conditions best predict whether increasingly capable AI systems remain corrigible, accountable, and broadly beneficial rather than concentrating power or producing catastrophic loss of control?

**Disciplines:** computer science, security engineering, economics, law, cognitive science, political theory, organizational safety, and sociology.

**Why it matters:** AI may alter the production of knowledge, power, labor, and strategic capability simultaneously.

**Possible truth gems:** measurable precursors of control failure; thresholds where oversight becomes ceremonial; governance mechanisms that remain effective under competitive pressure; ownership structures that change benefit distribution.

**Discriminating evidence:** incident databases, controlled agent evaluations, organizational audits, deployment natural experiments, and time-bounded forecasts. Claims must be updated rapidly because the system changes.

### 4. Climate, energy, and robust transition pathways

**Question:** Which regionally differentiated portfolio of mitigation, adaptation, energy abundance, ecosystem protection, and carbon removal minimizes expected human and ecological harm across plausible climate, technology, and political models?

**Disciplines:** climate science, ecology, engineering, economics, public health, political economy, development studies, and ethics.

**Why it matters:** The problem is global and long-lived, but costs, vulnerabilities, and feasible interventions are radically unequal.

**Possible truth gems:** robust no-regret interventions; infrastructure bottlenecks; adaptation limits; portfolio elements whose ranking changes most across assumptions; distributional conditions required for political durability.

**Discriminating evidence:** regional pilot outcomes, energy-system performance, climate attribution, policy natural experiments, and explicit value-of-information calculations.

### 5. Biological catastrophe prevention and legitimate preparedness

**Question:** Which layered defenses most reduce natural, accidental, and deliberate biological catastrophe while preserving scientific benefit, civil liberties, access, and international trust?

**Disciplines:** epidemiology, microbiology, security studies, public health, logistics, law, behavioral science, ethics, and international governance.

**Why it matters:** Rare biological events combine extreme stakes with uncertain probabilities and powerful dual-use tensions.

**Possible truth gems:** surveillance signals with high early-warning value; defense layers that remain useful across threat classes; trust-preserving emergency authorities; interventions vulnerable to catastrophic common-mode failure.

**Discriminating evidence:** outbreak retrospectives, exercises, system response times, blinded detection trials, and careful red-team studies under controlled disclosure.

### 6. Preventable chronic disease and healthy longevity

**Question:** Which modifiable exposures and interventions causally account for the largest preventable loss of healthy life across different populations, and which effects replicate outside the settings in which they were discovered?

**Disciplines:** medicine, epidemiology, nutrition, environmental science, economics, behavioral science, genetics, and urban design.

**Why it matters:** Large burdens accumulate from many interacting causes, while public guidance is vulnerable to confounding and commercial distortion.

**Possible truth gems:** high-confidence causal exposures; intervention heterogeneity; life-course windows with exceptional leverage; where observational and randomized evidence genuinely converge.

**Discriminating evidence:** triangulation across randomized trials, natural experiments, negative controls, mechanistic evidence, longitudinal cohorts, and replication across populations.

### 7. Childhood conditions, learning, and lifelong agency

**Question:** Which early-life conditions and educational practices causally improve long-term learning, health, agency, creativity, and social capacity across cultural and economic contexts?

**Disciplines:** developmental psychology, education, neuroscience, economics, anthropology, sociology, and public policy.

**Why it matters:** Early interventions can compound across a lifetime, but easily measured test scores are incomplete proxies for human development.

**Possible truth gems:** sensitive periods; portable relational and instructional mechanisms; tradeoffs among standardization, mastery, curiosity, and belonging; which effects persist into adulthood.

**Discriminating evidence:** long-horizon trials and natural experiments with multiple outcomes, cross-cultural replications, and explicit analysis of attrition and selection.

### 8. Economic security and autonomy under automation

**Question:** If automation materially reduces demand for human labor in some sectors, which combinations of ownership, income provision, public goods, labor institutions, and market structure preserve productivity, autonomy, dignity, and political legitimacy?

**Disciplines:** economics, political theory, history, sociology, law, psychology, and technology studies.

**Why it matters:** The distribution of technological gains may determine whether abundance produces emancipation, dependence, or instability.

**Possible truth gems:** ownership structures that dominate transfer-only approaches; conditions under which unconditional provision expands rather than reduces agency; legitimacy thresholds; transition policies robust to uncertain automation speed.

**Discriminating evidence:** policy experiments, historical technology transitions, firm-level automation data, distributional simulations, and prospective indicators registered before large transitions.

### 9. Information ecosystems, polarization, and free correction

**Question:** Which interventions improve belief accuracy, reduce destructive polarization, and preserve open disagreement without centralizing censorship or creating predictable backfire?

**Disciplines:** psychology, communications, network science, political science, computer science, law, and media studies.

**Why it matters:** Public reasoning fails when attention, identity, and information distribution reward confidence and antagonism over correction.

**Possible truth gems:** when accuracy prompts work; which network structures amplify false consensus; how friction, provenance, community notes, deliberation, and moderation differ; conditions where interventions backfire.

**Discriminating evidence:** platform experiments with independent oversight, longitudinal belief measures, cross-platform natural experiments, and audits of distributional and speech effects.

### 10. Meaning, connection, and durable human flourishing

**Question:** Which conditions causally support durable meaning, agency, belonging, psychological health, and prosocial functioning across cultures and life stages, and which apparent causes are actually consequences or class-specific proxies?

**Disciplines:** psychology, philosophy, anthropology, sociology, neuroscience, religious studies, economics, and the arts.

**Why it matters:** Societies often optimize measurable means—income, output, engagement—without a defensible model of the human ends they are supposed to serve.

**Possible truth gems:** universal versus culture-bound components of flourishing; causal importance of reciprocal obligation and belonging; relationships among pleasure, meaning, agency, and service; warning signs that a proxy is being optimized against the underlying good.

**Discriminating evidence:** cross-cultural longitudinal research, natural experiments, experience sampling, qualitative causal process tracing, intervention trials, and explicit measurement-invariance tests.

## 7. Why the first portfolio should span disciplines

If SES claims to be discipline-agnostic, it should not be validated only where one formalism already dominates. A diverse portfolio reveals translation failures and forces the protocol to handle quantitative data, historical evidence, testimony, mechanisms, forecasts, and value boundaries.

However, diversity should occur across the portfolio, not inside one unmanageably broad inquiry. Each inquiry needs a defined center of gravity and a bounded team. The program should also maintain same-domain benchmark series so that calibration can accumulate.

A strong initial portfolio contains:

- **three resolvable benchmark inquiries** with known or near-term outcomes;
- **three retrospective causal inquiries** analyzed under blinded historical cutoffs;
- **three live policy inquiries** with genuine decisions and monitoring plans; and
- **one frontier inquiry** designed to reveal the limits of the method.

## 8. Truth-gem extraction

A truth gem is not a striking sentence. It is a compact learning that survives serious variation in model, discipline, and assumptions while retaining explicit boundaries.

Each candidate gem must contain:

1. **Claim:** the shortest accurate statement.
2. **Type:** empirical regularity, causal mechanism, threshold, boundary condition, decision rule, bridge principle, or ignorance result.
3. **Scope:** populations, systems, places, and times to which it applies.
4. **Support:** independent evidence groups and models that converge.
5. **Confidence:** calibrated probability or qualitative interval with rationale.
6. **Invariance:** which perturbations it survives.
7. **Boundary:** where it stops being reliable.
8. **Disconfirmation:** observations that would weaken or overturn it.
9. **Action implication:** what changes if it is true.
10. **Portability:** other questions to which it may transfer.
11. **Version:** date, evidence cutoff, and revision history.

### Gem classes

- **Invariant:** a conclusion shared across competing plausible models.
- **Mechanism:** a repeatedly supported causal pathway.
- **Threshold:** a condition beyond which system behavior changes sharply.
- **Boundary gem:** a precise statement of where a popular claim fails.
- **Bridge gem:** a translation connecting constructs in different disciplines.
- **Decision gem:** an action that remains favorable across model uncertainty.
- **Ignorance gem:** a demonstrated reason the available evidence cannot identify an answer.
- **Question gem:** a newly discovered formulation that makes an apparently insoluble problem tractable.

An ignorance gem is a success when it replaces counterfeit certainty with a precise measurement or identification limit.

## 9. Truth-gem quality test

Before publication, ask:

- Is the gem more informative than the source summaries from which it was derived?
- Does it survive the strongest minority model?
- Are its scope and boundary short enough to state alongside it?
- Would a critic recognize their objection in the disconfirmation clause?
- Can another team reproduce its provenance?
- Does it generate a novel prediction, decision, or research direction?
- Is its rhetorical memorability proportional to, rather than greater than, its evidence?

If the boundary and disconfirmation conditions cannot accompany the claim, publish it as a conjecture, not a gem.

## 10. Recommended first flagship

The highest-leverage flagship topic is **collective intelligence and epistemic institutions**, because it directly tests SES while producing knowledge that can improve every subsequent inquiry.

The first executable flagship should be narrower:

> Across a preregistered set of resolvable forecasting and blinded retrospective questions, which combination of independent evidence collection, structured model advocacy, dependence mapping, adversarial review, and deterministic synthesis produces the best calibration and error correction per unit of time?

Competing arms should include conventional expert review, unstructured language-model research, prediction aggregation, structured analytic techniques, and SES variants. This would turn the founding philosophy into a falsifiable research program.

## 11. Final standard

The output at the end of the mineshaft is not a pile of summaries. It is a versioned gem ledger containing robust learnings, exact boundaries, live objections, and the shortest path to discovering that any gem is wrong.
