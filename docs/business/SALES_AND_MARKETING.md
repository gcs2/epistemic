# Sales and marketing

Part of [BIZ-001](README.md). This is an operating design and draft library. **No outreach, publication, advertising purchase, or customer contact is authorized by this document.**

## Initial acquisition sequence

1. Start with relevant people the founder can legitimately reach.
2. Hold problem interviews without assuming a sale.
3. Offer a bounded paid pilot to qualified prospects.
4. Ask satisfied customers for feedback, a referral, and separately for permission to publish a case study.
5. Expand into relevant communities, partnerships, useful public demonstrations, or marketplaces if the first channel is insufficient.
6. Consider paid acquisition only after estimating delivery contribution and an affordable acquisition cost.

This is a proposed order, not evidence that any channel will work. Avoid building a large website, content factory, or bulk outreach system before testing a clear offer.

## Pipeline definitions

| Stage | Required evidence / exit condition |
|---|---|
| Candidate | Reason this organization might fit; lawful, appropriate contact route |
| Contact approved | Founder approves recipient, channel, and message |
| Contacted | Actual send date and message version recorded privately |
| Discovery | Conversation completed; problem and current workaround documented |
| Qualified | Concrete need, plausible budget owner, delivery fit, and next step |
| Proposal | Scope, fee, acceptance test, and terms actually sent |
| Contracted | Authorized agreement accepted; payment requirements explicit |
| Paid pilot active | Required starting payment received and access approved |
| Delivered | Agreed deliverables and acceptance process completed |
| Closed / repeat | Payment state, outcome, support obligations, repeat/referral evidence |
| Lost / paused | Reason recorded; no invented future revenue |

Track each opportunity in an appropriate private record. The public [tracker](TRACKER.md) contains only aggregate, non-identifying evidence and next decisions. Preserve denominators: five replies out of ten delivered messages is not five out of all prospects ever considered.

## Draft outreach: problem discovery

> Hi [name]—I'm exploring whether preparing recurring client reports creates avoidable manual work for [relevant team type]. Could you walk me through how you handled your last reporting cycle in a short conversation? I'm testing whether a small workflow-improvement service would be useful; I don't yet know whether it fits your situation. No problem if this isn't relevant.

Personalize only from legitimate context. Do not imply a prior relationship, fabricate praise, or conceal the commercial purpose. Final message, recipient, channel, and applicable outreach requirements need review before sending.

## Draft outreach: after confirmed fit

> Based on the workflow we discussed, I can propose a small pilot covering [one workflow]. We would record the starting process, deliver [specific change], and test it against [agreed measure]. The fee, access needed, support window, and limits would be written down before work begins. Would you like a scoped proposal?

## Follow-up and objections

Agree a limited follow-up approach before launch; stop when asked and maintain suppression privately. No bulk scraping or autonomous sequences by default.

- “We already have a tool”: inspect the unresolved gap; do not sell a duplicate.
- “We cannot share the data”: explore redacted or synthetic examples; decline if safe delivery is impossible.
- “Can you guarantee savings?”: offer a defined test and deliverable, not an unsupported outcome.
- “Too expensive”: examine value, scope, and alternatives; do not discount below full-cost viability without recording a deliberate learning subsidy.
- “Not now”: record the reason and only a permission-based next contact.

## Marketing assets and proof

Create only what helps the next sale: a one-page offer, a clearly labeled demonstration, a scope example, and answers to actual buyer questions. Case studies require customer permission and should include context, baseline, intervention, measurement period, costs, limitations, and failures. Synthetic demos must remain labeled.

Keep a message/version log with audience, channel, effort, cash cost, delivered contacts, qualified conversations, proposals, paid starts, and reasons lost. Report counts alongside rates. Attribute acquisition costs consistently; referrals and founder time are not automatically free.

A marketing win is a qualified buyer taking a useful next step—not merely a high-ranking post or broad attention. Public cultural significance remains a possible later outcome, not a substitute for customer benefit.
