# Customers and offers

Part of [BIZ-001](README.md). All segments below are **unvalidated candidates**, not market findings or an approved launch.

## Candidate starting points

| Candidate | Buyer and concrete job | Evidence to seek | Main obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurring client-report preparation | Small agency owner or operations lead; assemble and check recurring reports | Recent reporting cycles, handling time, error/rework history, exports | Sensitive client data; existing tools may already solve it |
| Reconciliation and exception handling | Small service-business operator; compare records and resolve mismatches | Recurring examples, current workaround, cost of missed exceptions | Integration burden; avoid regulated financial advice or unsupervised money movement |
| Software/workflow diagnosis and repair | Technical or operational owner; resolve a reproducible recurring failure | Reproduction steps, logs, frequency, acceptance test | One-off firefighting may not repeat; skill and access requirements |
| Narrow evidence brief | Product or operations lead; answer a specific decision question | Upcoming decision, sources, deadline, current research process | Value harder to attribute; generic research is easy to substitute |

Working default to investigate: recurring client-report preparation, **only if we can reach appropriate buyers**. No chosen market has been established. If the founder has substantially better access or relevant skill elsewhere, test that advantage first.

## Choose without false precision

Record evidence for: reachable buyers, recurring pain, identifiable budget authority, observable outcome, safe data access, delivery competence, bounded integration effort, repeatability, and sales-cycle fit with runway. Mark each as supported, contradicted, or unknown and link the record.

Treat data-access permission, delivery competence, and a testable outcome as gates, not weak scores that can be averaged away. Do not manufacture numerical success probabilities from intuition.

## Discovery conversation

Ask about past behavior before proposing a solution:

1. Walk me through the last time you did this task.
2. How often does it happen, who works on it, and what goes wrong?
3. What have you already tried or bought? What remains unresolved?
4. Can we inspect a redacted example and the current workflow?
5. Who approves changes and spending? What constraints or deadlines matter?
6. What result would make a small pilot worthwhile, and how could we measure it?
7. If the fit is real, what would be needed to agree a paid pilot?

Record the customer's account separately from our interpretation. Ask before recording a call. Log objections, refusals, and successful existing alternatives, not only positive reactions. A hypothetical willingness-to-pay answer is weaker than an accepted, funded scope.

## First offer: draft, not a published promise

**Working offer:** “We review one recurring reporting workflow, identify the avoidable manual work, and implement and test one agreed improvement.”

Possible scope:

- One defined workflow and agreed input sources.
- A baseline sample and a written diagnosis.
- One bounded change, tested against agreed examples.
- A plain-language handover, limitations, rollback instructions, and an agreed support window.

Exclude unspecified integrations, unlimited revisions, ongoing operations, guaranteed savings, and high-stakes automated decisions. If the diagnosis finds no worthwhile change, the agreed diagnostic deliverable can still be completed; never imply that implementation or savings are guaranteed.

Use a paid diagnostic when uncertainty makes a fixed implementation promise unsafe. Credit toward implementation, milestone billing, and other commercial terms are options to negotiate, not adopted policy.

## Offer test

Before sending proposals, record the segment, offer version, price basis, founder time cap, cash-spend cap, review date, qualification criteria, and stop/revise conditions in [the tracker](TRACKER.md). No default spending authority is implied.

A reasonable first discovery batch is five qualified conversations, followed by a review; this is a learning batch, not a representative survey or statistical proof. Choose an alternative batch if access, cost, or diversity warrants it.

Use the [opportunity card](../../templates/business/OPPORTUNITY_CARD.md) privately, then the [pilot brief](../../templates/business/PILOT_BRIEF.md) once fit is demonstrated.
