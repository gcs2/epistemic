# Positioning and language

Part of [BIZ-001](README.md). Public brand is undecided. SES remains the internal name for Syncretic Epistemic Synthesis; an internal method name need not become the customer-facing offer.

## Three distinct layers

- **Customer offer:** a specific problem and useful deliverable.
- **Business identity:** the eventual public brand; choose after testing comprehension and fit.
- **Internal method and engine:** SES and the versioned reference implementation.

Explain the customer outcome first, the process second, and the technical method when relevant. Plain language must preserve limitations, not conceal them. Disclose material AI use and data processing arrangements before receiving customer information.

## Translation guide

These are communication aids, not assertions that technical concepts are identical.

| Internal term | Customer-facing explanation |
|---|---|
| Epistemic synthesis | Review the evidence and explain what it supports |
| Provenance / source dependence | Check where claims came from and whether sources repeat one another |
| Hypothesis space | The possible explanations we are considering—and what may be missing |
| Value of information | What is worth checking next before spending more |
| Bounded decision assurance | Show the assumptions, risks, and checks behind a recommendation |
| Robustness analysis | See whether the recommendation changes under plausible assumptions |
| Truth gem | A useful finding, with the evidence and its limits |
| Agentic harness | A research workflow with automated steps and human checks |
| Recursive self-improvement | Use measured results to improve the process |
| SES engine | Experimental analysis software supporting the work |

## Draft external copy

**Headline:** “Less manual work in your recurring client reports.”

**Description:** “We review one reporting workflow, identify where time and rework accumulate, and agree a small improvement to test. You receive the changes, documentation, and a comparison against the starting workflow.”

**Invitation:** “Show us how you prepared the last report. We can discuss whether a small pilot makes sense.”

These are drafts for the reporting candidate, not an assertion of demonstrated savings or launched services. If another segment wins, replace the concrete task rather than using a vague universal intelligence pitch.

## Claims discipline

| Claim | Present status | Requirement before use |
|---|---|---|
| An experimental SES reference engine exists | Supported by repository artifacts | Link version and [method boundary](../../README.md) |
| We offer a scoped workflow review | Proposed offer | Confirm owner, capacity, actual scope, and terms before publication |
| This customer saved a specific number of hours | Not established | Defined baseline, observation window, measurement, caveats, and publication permission |
| SES is better than ordinary expert work | Not established | Appropriate comparison with costs and independent validation |
| Guaranteed truth, guaranteed ROI, autonomous discovery | Not justified | Do not use these promises |

Do not convert a synthetic finding into a customer-success claim. A paid invoice establishes a transaction, not efficacy. A before/after improvement alone does not isolate SES's contribution.

## Message testing

Ask target users what they think the offer does, who it serves, what they would receive, and what they find unclear. Record their unaided answers before explaining.

Test one substantial message change at a time where practical. Track qualified replies, conversations, and accepted offers—not impressions alone. Small or non-random samples support local learning, not claims that one message universally wins.
